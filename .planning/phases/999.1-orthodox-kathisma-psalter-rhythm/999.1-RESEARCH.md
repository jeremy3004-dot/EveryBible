# Phase 999.1: Orthodox Kathisma Psalter Rhythm - Research

**Researched:** 2026-04-09
**Status:** Backlog discovery
**Confidence:** High on liturgical shape, medium on the exact product surface until text/licensing is chosen

## Why this is a backlog item

Kathisma is not just "another Psalms plan." It is a liturgical structure with Orthodox-specific text, numbering, cadence, and seasonal rules. That means the product question is not only "can the app show Psalms in chunks?" but "which parts of the Orthodox practice are we promising to preserve?"

The right move is to park this as a GSD backlog item until we decide whether EveryBible wants:

1. A simple Psalms-based devotional plan inspired by kathisma
2. A faithful Orthodox Psalter rhythm with liturgical expectations

Those are related, but they are not the same feature.

## What a kathisma is

- A kathisma is one twentieth of the Psalter.
- Each kathisma is split into three sections called stases.
- The Greek root means "sit"; the OCA notes that a kathisma is a portion of the Psalter readable in one sitting/session.
- In Orthodox worship, the Psalter is appointed across the Daily Offices, not treated only as a private reading checklist.

Key sourced facts:

- The Orthodox Church in America defines a kathisma as one twentieth of the Psalter and says each kathisma is subdivided into three stases:
  https://www.oca.org/liturgics/outlines/the-division-of-the-psalter-into-kathismas
- OrthodoxWiki notes the Psalter is divided into 20 kathismata, each into three stases, with Kathisma XVII occupied entirely by Psalm 118/119 because of its length:
  https://en.orthodoxwiki.org/Psalms
- St. Tikhon's liturgical books page gives the full kathisma-to-psalm mapping and explicitly shows both Hebrew and LXX numbering:
  https://www.sttikhonsmonastery.org/article/Liturgical%2BBooks

## What makes a kathisma feature faithful vs unfaithful

### Faithful elements

- Orthodox Psalter structure: 20 kathismata, 3 stases each
- Orthodox text tradition: preferably Septuagint-based Psalter, not a Protestant/Masoretic-only Psalms rendering
- Orthodox numbering awareness: LXX and modern Protestant numbering do not always line up one-to-one
- Liturgical cadence: outside Great Lent, the Psalter is typically completed in one week through Vespers and Matins; in Great Lent, it is accelerated and read twice weekly
- Prayer-rule context: many people also read a kathisma privately at home with opening/closing prayers or an abbreviated rule

### Unfaithful shortcuts

- "20 days of Psalms" without kathisma naming or stasis boundaries
- Reusing MT-only Psalm numbering without warning Orthodox users
- Treating kathisma as a static modern streak plan while ignoring the weekly and Lenten cycles
- Omitting the Great Lent adjustment while still labeling the feature as a liturgical kathisma cycle

## The most important product truth

The current EveryBible scripture stack is not yet an Orthodox Psalter stack.

Today the app is centered on BSB and other modern Bible content, while Orthodox kathisma practice is tied to the Septuagint Psalter tradition. The app can absolutely support a kathisma feature, but the fidelity level depends on the text choice:

- If we keep current Psalms text/numbering only, we can build a kathisma-inspired devotional feature.
- If we want to call it a faithful Orthodox kathisma rhythm, we should add an Orthodox Psalter source with LXX-aware numbering and ideally the traditional kathisma/stasis boundaries baked into the content.

This is the biggest hidden requirement.

Supporting sources:

- OrthodoxWiki states that the Orthodox Church uses the Septuagint Psalter and highlights the numbering differences:
  https://en.orthodoxwiki.org/Psalms
- The OCA kathisma page says it is advantageous to read from the Septuagint version of the Psalter while also providing Hebrew numbering:
  https://www.oca.org/liturgics/outlines/the-division-of-the-psalter-into-kathismas

## What the online research says about actual usage

### Ordinary weeks

OrthodoxWiki gives the standard year-round kathisma appointments for Matins and Vespers outside Great Lent. The key product implication is that this is not a flat one-kathisma-per-day plan. The assignment changes by weekday and by service.

Source:
https://en.orthodoxwiki.org/Psalms

### Great Lent

OrthodoxWiki also gives the intensified Great Lent cycle, where kathismata are added to the Hours and the Psalter is read through twice each week. This means a truly faithful "current kathisma" mode needs season awareness and special-case rules.

Source:
https://en.orthodoxwiki.org/Psalms

### Private/home prayer use

Parish-level Orthodox practice often adapts kathisma reading into a home prayer rule. Saint Demetrios describes reading one kathisma daily during Lent, notes that each kathisma takes about 20 minutes, and explicitly tells people they can read it at any time of day and simply continue the next day if they miss one. That is pastoral and app-friendly.

Source:
https://stdemetrios.net/psalter

This matters because it opens a practical compromise: the app can offer a faithful liturgical mode and a home-rule mode without pretending they are identical.

## Competitive/reference landscape

There are already Orthodox-focused Psalter products that anchor themselves around kathisma groupings and public-domain LXX text. One simple example is psalter.app, which says it uses Orthodox kathisma groupings and public-domain Brenton/LXX2012/KJV text.

Source:
https://psalter.app/about/

Implication: users who want a stripped-down Orthodox Psalter experience already expect:

- Kathisma grouping
- Orthodox-compatible text
- Low-friction reading
- No heavy feature clutter

## Best product framing options

## Option A: Add it as a reading plan

**What it would be**

- A single plan such as "Psalter by Kathisma"
- Likely 20 days, one kathisma per day
- Each day could show 3 collapsible stases

**Pros**

- Fastest path
- Reuses existing plan enrollment/progress UI almost directly
- Works offline with current plan infrastructure
- Easier to explain to non-Orthodox users

**Cons**

- Not faithful to the actual weekly and Lenten liturgical cycle
- Easy to mislabel if called the Orthodox kathisma cycle without caveats
- Still blocked by the Orthodox text/numbering issue if we want full fidelity

**Verdict**

Good as a devotional adaptation, not good as the final faithful Orthodox implementation.

## Option B: Add it as a rhythm preset

**What it would be**

- A special preset rhythm: "Orthodox Kathisma Rhythm"
- Could expose one of several modes:
  - Home Rule: one kathisma per session/day
  - Weekly Office Cycle: current weekday/service-based kathisma set
  - Great Lent Cycle: intensified schedule

**Why this fits the app better**

- The app already has a rhythm abstraction
- Kathisma is inherently about recurring cadence, not just finite completion
- Rhythm language matches Orthodox practice better than "finish this plan in N days"
- The same user may want both a simple private rule and a liturgical schedule

**Cons**

- Still needs a text and numbering strategy
- Needs more UI explanation than a normal plan
- Truly faithful live scheduling needs seasonal logic

**Verdict**

This is the best long-term shape if the goal is to be faithful rather than merely inspired.

## Option C: Two-layer approach (recommended)

### Layer 1: devotional on-ramp

- Ship a simple plan first:
  - "Kathisma in 20 Sessions" or
  - "Pray the Psalter by Kathisma"
- Use clear language that this is a private-rule adaptation
- Include kathisma and stasis labels

### Layer 2: faithful Orthodox rhythm

- Later add an Orthodox Psalter rhythm mode
- Back it with LXX-aware text and numbering
- Support ordinary-week and Great-Lent scheduling
- Potentially add "service context" labels such as Vespers / Matins / Hours

**Why this is the safest recommendation**

- It avoids overclaiming fidelity on day one
- It creates value quickly with existing infrastructure
- It leaves room for a stronger Orthodox product surface later

## Recommended implementation path for EveryBible

## Recommendation

If Peter wants this added faithfully, **treat kathisma as a rhythm-centered Orthodox Psalter feature, but ship it in two passes**:

1. **Pass 1**
   - Add an Orthodox-facing Psalter content pack or dataset decision first
   - Ship a private-rule kathisma plan or preset rhythm
   - Explicitly label it as a home prayer rule / personal cadence

2. **Pass 2**
   - Add liturgical scheduling logic
   - Add ordinary-week and Great-Lent modes
   - Add stasis-level progress and "continue where I left off" within each kathisma

## Concrete shape inside the current app

### MVP shape

- New plan or preset rhythm title: `Orthodox Kathisma`
- New metadata fields:
  - `tradition: orthodox`
  - `text_family: lxx`
  - `cadence_mode: home-rule | weekly-office | great-lent`
- New copy rules:
  - Use `Kathisma I-XX`
  - Use `Stasis 1-3`
  - Do not market it as "Psalms in 20 Days"

### Better-than-MVP shape

- New dedicated Psalter surface under Plans or More
- Toggle:
  - `Home rule`
  - `Liturgical week`
  - `Great Lent`
- Reading cards show:
  - Kathisma number
  - Stasis breakdown
  - Psalm crosswalk if app text numbering differs
  - Optional opening/closing prayer helpers

## Data/modeling implications

### If implemented as a plan

- Each day becomes one kathisma
- Each `ReadingPlanEntry` day may need multiple Psalm ranges
- Current plan entry shape can hold that, but the display layer needs to group those ranges under three stases
- This likely argues for a small Psalter-specific display model rather than overloading generic plan copy

### If implemented as a rhythm

- Add a system preset rhythm that the composer UI cannot accidentally corrupt
- Rhythm day/session should resolve to:
  - one kathisma in home-rule mode
  - one or more service-appointed kathismata in liturgical mode
- `ReadingPlanRhythmSessionSegment` is already close to what we need, but we would probably add:
  - `label` (Kathisma XII)
  - `subsegments` or `stases`
  - `liturgicalContext` (Matins, Vespers, First Hour, etc.)

## Hidden engineering requirements

1. **Orthodox text decision**
   - Need a clear source for an Orthodox-compatible Psalter text and numbering policy

2. **Psalm numbering crosswalk**
   - The UI may need to show both modern and LXX numbering, or at least normalize quietly but accurately

3. **Season engine**
   - A faithful live cycle needs date + season awareness
   - Great Lent and special periods are not optional edge cases if we call the feature faithful

4. **Resume granularity**
   - Kathisma is often read in one sitting, but users will still stop mid-stasis
   - Resume should support sub-kathisma progress, not only whole-day completion

5. **Terminology and onboarding**
   - Non-Orthodox users will not know words like kathisma or stasis
   - Orthodox users will care if these are diluted or mislabeled

## Product recommendation in one sentence

**Do not ship kathisma as just another Psalms challenge. Ship it either as a clearly labeled private-rule adaptation or as a rhythm-based Orthodox Psalter feature with LXX-aware text and liturgical scheduling.**

## Suggested next GSD move

When this is ready to leave backlog, promote it as a real phase and split it like this:
