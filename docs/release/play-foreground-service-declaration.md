Behaviour worth knowing before you fill in the form (from
`patches/expo-media-control+1.0.12.patch` and `src/services/audio/androidMediaSession.ts`):

- The service starts only when the user starts Bible audio. It returns `START_NOT_STICKY` and
  is **not** restarted after boot or after the process dies, even though the library
  manifest requests `RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED`.
- It stops when playback is stopped and when the app is swiped away (`onTaskRemoved`).
- expo-av still does the playback. The service only keeps the process alive and hosts the
  MediaSession and its media-style notification.

**Also check the dataSync declaration.** The merged manifest has contained a `dataSync`
foreground service since the background downloader was added. If Play Console shows only
the media-playback type, or none at all, that needs a declaration too. A suggested entry is
at the end of this file. Confirm both on the next AAB with:

```
bundletool dump manifest --bundle=app-release.aab | grep -E "uses-permission|foregroundServiceType"
```

## Declaration text: FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MEDIA_PLAYBACK

**Foreground service type:** Media playback

**Use case (pick from Play's list):** Media playback. This covers audio that keeps playing in
the background, including streamed audio.

**Describe the app functionality that uses this foreground service type**

> Every Bible plays audio recordings of the Bible, one chapter at a time, and moves on to
> the next chapter automatically. Listening usually lasts several minutes to an hour. The
> user starts it by tapping Listen (play) on a chapter or in a reading plan. Many users listen
> with the screen off, for example while commuting or at night with the sleep timer, so
> playback has to continue while the app is in the background.
>
> While audio is playing, the app runs a media playback foreground service with a
> MediaSession. It shows a media-style notification and lock-screen player with the book
> and chapter, the translation name, play/pause, previous/next chapter, a seek bar and
> 10-second skip. Wired and Bluetooth headset buttons also work. The service starts only
> when the user presses play. It stops when the user stops playback, when the chapter
> sequence ends, or when the user swipes the app away. The app never starts it on its own,
> after boot, or to do any work other than play audio the user asked for.

**Describe the impact on the user if the task is deferred or interrupted by the system**

> If the system delays or stops the service, the Bible audio the user started stops
> partway through a chapter. The screen is usually off at that moment, so the user
> doesn't find out until they notice the silence, and then they have to unlock the phone,
> reopen the app and find their place again. The notification and lock-screen controls
> (pause, next or previous chapter, seek) would also disappear, and the headset buttons
> would stop working. Deferring the start is not acceptable either: the user has just
> pressed play and expects to hear audio immediately.

**Why a foreground service (not WorkManager or a job)**

> This is continuous playback that the user can hear, starts, and controls. Android's
> guidance for this is a media playback foreground service with a MediaSession. Deferrable
> background work APIs cannot keep audio playing, and they do not provide lock-screen or
> headset media controls.

**User-facing feature description** (for the listing, help text or reviewer notes)

> Listen with your screen off. Start any chapter and Every Bible keeps reading aloud when
> you lock your phone or switch apps. Control playback from the lock screen, the
> notification or your headphones: pause, skip back or forward 10 seconds, or jump to the
> previous or next chapter. With discreet mode on, the lock screen shows only "Now playing",
> with no book or translation name.

## Demo video: what to record

Google asks for a short video showing the steps a user takes to trigger the feature. Record
one continuous screen capture (plus camera footage of the headset step if you like) on a
real Android 14+ phone running the release build (`production` or `preview` profile, not
Expo Go or a dev client). Aim for 60–90 seconds, then upload it to YouTube as unlisted or to
Drive with link sharing, and paste the link into the form.

1. **Launch** Every Bible from the home screen. You can already be signed in; sign-in is not
   needed for audio.
2. **Open a chapter** (for example John 1) in the Bible tab and **tap Listen / play**. Audio
   starts, and the listening screen shows playback progress.
3. **Pull down the notification shade.** Show the Every Bible media notification with the
   book and chapter ("John 1"), the translation name and the artwork.
4. **Use the notification controls.** Pause, then play, then tap next chapter. Show the title
   change to "John 2".
5. **Lock the phone** with the power button, then wake the screen. The lock-screen player
   shows the chapter and controls, and **audio keeps playing** (make sure the recording
   captures sound). Drag the seek bar or press skip 10 seconds.
6. **Leave it locked for a short while** (cut or speed up the footage and say so in a
   caption) to show playback continuing with the screen off.
7. **Unlock and switch to another app** (for example the home screen or Chrome). Audio keeps
   playing, and the notification is still there.
8. **Optional:** press play/pause on Bluetooth or wired headphones to show headset control.
9. **Stop.** Return to Every Bible and stop playback. Show that **the notification
   disappears**, so the service ends when the user stops listening.
10. **Optional:** start playback again, then swipe Every Bible away from Recents. Audio stops
    and the notification is removed.

Tips: turn on "Show taps" in Developer options so reviewers can follow each step. Use the
English interface. Keep the device clock and status bar visible so the elapsed time with the
screen locked is clear.

## Suggested entry for the existing FOREGROUND_SERVICE_DATA_SYNC (if not already declared)

**Use case:** Network transfer (download) started by the user. Note that on Android 14+ this
library uses user-initiated data transfer jobs instead of the `dataSync` service.

> The user can download a Bible translation's audio (a whole Bible, a testament or a book)
> or its text for offline use. Downloads are large (hundreds of MB) and start only when the
> user taps Download. On Android 13 and below, a dataSync foreground service keeps the
> download running and shows its progress while the app is in the background. If it is
> deferred or interrupted, the download stalls partway through and the user cannot read or
> listen offline until they reopen the app and try again.

**Video:** open the translation picker → open a translation's menu → Download audio (or
text) → show the progress → go to the home screen → show that the download continues and
finishes.
